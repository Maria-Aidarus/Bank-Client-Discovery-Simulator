### What went well

- Opened with “What are you saving for?” and established the certification goal, the QAR 9,000 target, the 18-month horizon, and that it must stay separate from the emergency fund.
- Used the unexpected car-bill question to surface that a repair could consume most of the emergency savings and that Mohamed would rather pause certification savings than dip into emergency money.
- Explicitly tested the Goal Saver withdrawal delay; the client confirmed several business days is workable for the planned certification expense but not for the same-day emergency reserve.
- Tested contribution flexibility and confirmed he wants to lower or pause contributions in expensive months. The summary and recommendations correctly use Everyday Access for emergency money and Goal Saver for certification only.

### What was missed

- No month-by-month affordability check was completed: he never asked for take-home pay, fixed bills, or the monthly surplus, so the average QAR 500 contribution implied by QAR 9,000 over 18 months was not tested. The summary flags affordability as something to check, but the transcript does not establish it.
- The current emergency-savings balance and the size of the reserve were not established. The student learned a repair could use most of the emergency savings but cannot judge whether the Everyday Access reserve is adequate or how much needs building.
- Irregular expenses were only partially explored. Family travel appeared only in the final answer, and there was no follow-up to estimate these costs or plan how to set money aside before committing to goal transfers.
- The client twice asked how early withdrawal would affect the goal bonus and target date. The student did not follow up or explain that consequence, leaving an important Goal Saver trade-off unresolved.

### How to improve

- Ask for take-home pay, fixed bills, groceries, and typical irregular costs, then calculate whether QAR 500 per month is realistic; if not, adjust the goal amount or date.
- Ask for the current emergency-savings balance and how many months of essential bills it covers, then size the Everyday Access reserve separately before setting goal transfers.
- Clarify the Goal Saver early-withdrawal terms: exactly when the bonus is forfeited and how a pause or withdrawal changes the target date, with a review or catch-up step after expensive months.
- Explain the trade-off directly: Everyday Access gives same-day access but no goal structure, while Goal Saver gives structure and adjustable transfers but delayed access and possible bonus loss, so the split should reflect that.
